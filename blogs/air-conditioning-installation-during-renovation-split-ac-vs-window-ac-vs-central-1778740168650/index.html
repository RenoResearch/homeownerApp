Air Conditioning Installation During Renovation: Split AC vs Window AC vs Central Cooling

Compare cooling options for your Singapore HDB renovation and choose the best fit for your home and budget

7 min read 0 views 14 May 2026

Choosing the Right Air Conditioning System for Your Singapore Renovation Singapore's tropical heat makes air conditioning essential, not optional. During renovation is the perfect time to upgrade your cooling system or switch to something more efficient. Let's compare your options. Option 1: Split Air Conditioning Units (Most Popular) What it is: A modern air conditioning system with an outdoor unit and one or more indoor wall-mounted units connected by refrigerant pipes. Cost breakdown for a 4-room HDB: - Unit 1 (bedroom/living room): $1,800 to $3,500 - Unit 2 (second bedroom): $1,500 to $2,800 - Unit 3 (third bedroom): $1,500 to $2,800 - Installation: $800 to $1,500 - Total: $5,600 to $10,600 for 3 units Monthly electricity cost: $80 to $150 depending on usage and efficiency rating Advantages: - Individual room control (each unit can be set to different temperatures) - Modern inverter technology saves up to 40% electricity compared to old units - Quiet operation (outdoor noise is minimal) - Takes up little wall space - Easy to maintain and clean filters - Can add more units later if needed Disadvantages: - Higher upfront cost compared to window units - Requires professional installation - Outdoor unit placement can be restricted in some HDB flats - Pipes running down exterior walls may not be aesthetically pleasing Best for: Most HDB homeowners, especially those wanting room-by-room control and energy efficiency. Option 2: Window Air Conditioning Units What it is: A compact unit that sits in your window frame, drawing in outside air and expelling hot air. Cost breakdown for a 4-room HDB: - Window AC unit (2.0 HP): $400 to $800 each - Installation brackets and weatherproofing: $100 to $200 per unit - Total for 3 units: $1,500 to $3,000 Monthly electricity cost: $120 to $200 for 1 unit running continuously (less efficient than split AC) Advantages: - Lowest upfront cost - No installation required (just fit into window frame) - Good for temporary cooling solutions - Easy to remove and take with you when you move - No contractor installation needed Disadvantages: - Blocks your window and natural light - Single unit cools the entire flat unevenly - Noisier operation (outdoor noise enters room) - Higher electricity consumption (older technology) - Limited to flats with suitable windows - Takes up valuable window space Best for: Temporary solutions, rental flats, or budget-conscious homeowners willing to sacrifice efficiency. Option 3: Central Air Conditioning (VRF Systems) What it is: A sophisticated system where one powerful outdoor unit connects to multiple indoor units throughout the flat, with individual temperature control in each room. Cost breakdown for a 4-room HDB: - System cost: $8,000 to $15,000 - Installation: $1,500 to $3,000 - Total: $9,500 to $18,000 Monthly electricity cost: $70 to $130 (most efficient option due to heat recovery technology) Advantages: - Highest energy efficiency (heat recovery system reduces wastage) - Individual room temperature control - Quietest operation (very low noise) - Compact indoor units don't protrude as much - Looks more premium and modern - Can integrate with smart home systems - Best for open-concept layouts Disadvantages: - Highest upfront investment - Requires skilled technicians for installation and maintenance - More complex system (higher repair costs if something breaks) - Overkill for small flats or limited budgets - Requires dedicated circuit breaker Best for: Larger flats, open-concept designs, energy-conscious homeowners willing to invest upfront for long-term savings. Efficiency Ratings: What to Look For All air conditioners in Singapore are rated with an energy efficiency ratio (EER). Higher EER means lower electricity bills. - EER below 8: Outdated, very inefficient - EER 8-9: Standard efficiency - EER 9-10: Good efficiency, recommended - EER 10+: Premium efficiency, highest cost but best long-term savings A 5-star inverter split AC with EER 11 can save you $400-$600 per year compared to a basic 2-star unit. Additional Cooling Solutions Worth Considering 1. Ceiling Fans with Air Conditioning Combining ceiling fans with AC can improve air circulation and reduce AC dependency. Cost: $150 to $400 per fan. 2. Outdoor Unit Placement During renovation, invest in proper insulation and shading for your outdoor unit. An AC unit in direct sunlight works 15-20% harder, consuming more electricity. 3. Smart Thermostats Pair your AC with a smart thermostat that learns your schedule and adjusts automatically. Cost: $300 to $800. Potential savings: $50-$100 per month. Maintenance Tips to Keep Your System Efficient 1. Clean the filter every 2 weeks during heavy use 2. Get professional servicing twice yearly (before summer and after winter) 3. Keep indoor and outdoor units clear of obstructions 4. Check refrigerant levels annually 5. Ensure proper insulation around pipes to prevent heat loss Renovation Planning Tips for Air Conditioning Before renovation: - Decide which rooms need cooling (not all rooms need individual AC) - Plan outdoor unit placement and ensure neighbor approval - Consider future expansion (build in extra capacity) - Coordinate AC installation with electrical work During renovation: - Install AC piping through walls before painting and tiling - Run extra electrical circuits if upgrading to a more powerful system - Ensure outdoor unit area is clear of obstacles - Test the system before construction ends Financial Considerations Government Incentives: - Some energy-efficient AC units qualify for SPRING Singapore grants or rebates (check current programs) - Check if your model qualifies for PUB water efficiency grants (some combo units) Financing Options: - Many contractors offer 12-24 month interest-free installment plans - HDB renovation loans can sometimes cover AC costs - Some credit cards offer cashback for appliance purchases Final Recommendation For most Singapore HDB homeowners, a quality split AC system with 3 units (for 3 bedrooms) is the best balance of cost, efficiency, and comfort. Inverter-based systems with EER 9-10 will provide excellent cooling while keeping electricity bills reasonable. If budget allows and you have an open-concept layout, consider a VRF central system for maximum efficiency. If you're temporary or budget-conscious, window AC units are adequate but less efficient in the long run.
